The reason nothing worked

There's a hormone your body is
supposed to make enough of.

It's called GLP-1. Its entire job is to tell your brain you're full.

When you eat, GLP-1 is released. It slows how quickly food leaves your stomach, it steadies your blood sugar so you don't spike and crash, and it sends one simple message upstairs: you've had enough.

In people who struggle with weight, that signal misfires. The food doesn't stop talking. You finish a meal and forty minutes later you're standing in the kitchen again, and you assume that's a character flaw.

It isn't. It's a signal problem.

GLP-1 medication doesn't fight your hunger. It restores the signal your body was supposed to be sending all along. Which is why patients describe the result the same way, almost word for word:

"For the first time in my adult life, I just… wasn't thinking about food."

That's not willpower. That's biology doing what it was always meant to do. And because it's a prescription medication, it comes with something a supplement never will — a medical team watching your dose and your progress every step of the way.
